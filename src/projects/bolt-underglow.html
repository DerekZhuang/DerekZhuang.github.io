On BOLT's Controls subteam I took on the under-glow board, which drives addressable LED strips on the bike and connects to its CAN bus. It extends the RGB lighting work I did for my dorm. My first Altium board was a guided 7-segment display tutorial that was never fabricated.

I drew the power regulation and microcontroller sheets, and the top-level sheet splits the board into six blocks Fig. 1. BOLT did not set a tight size limit because the bike has open space, so I put repairability and easy integration ahead of minimum size.

Design review

The Oct 22 power stage, rechecked on Oct 31. A senior BOLT member reviewed it with me before and after an AI-assisted datasheet check. I made the fixes myself.

1. Q1 gate. Q1 is a P-channel FET that blocks reverse polarity, with its gate tied to ground Fig. 4. The TVS diode can let the input reach 29.2 V before it clamps, but the NVTFS5116PL's gate-to-source rating is ±20 V, so a surge could destroy the FET. I added a Zener to limit the gate voltage.

2. 12 V strips. On Oct 29 I added a 12 V output option with two more strip outputs. The first version powered them from the unprotected input, so the fuse, reverse-polarity FET, and TVS did not cover them. I moved them onto the protected +12V_IN net.

3. Buck input capacitance. U4's input had only C7 (4.7 µF) and C8 (100 nF) Fig. 5. TI's datasheet recommends 10 µF or more of input decoupling, rated for twice the maximum input voltage to allow for ceramic derating, so one 4.7 µF part at 12 V gave too little. I added input capacitance.

Still open. The LED data lines run straight from the 3.3 V microcontroller to the strips with no level shifter, which I still need to confirm on the real strips.

Status. All three fixes are in the schematic, and I routed the board myself in Altium.

PCB layout

Routed by me in Altium Designer from the corrected schematic. Finished Nov 6, 2026 with zero design rule violations.

Four layers, 2.8 by 2.8 in, with all 50 parts on the top side.

Signals run on the top and bottom layers, with a ground layer and a power layer between them.

Power enters along the top edge, where the input protection and the buck converter sit. The STM32 is in the middle. The two CAN connectors are on the left edge, the two 12 V LED outputs on the right, and the two 5 V outputs along the bottom Fig. 6.

Checks before ordering. On Nov 5 the design rule check still reported 153 violations, and I worked them down to zero. A final review before ordering, again AI-assisted, caught that the board title and output labels were on the top copper layer instead of silkscreen. Under solder mask they would have been nearly invisible, and the copper lettering sat close to signal traces, so I moved it to silkscreen.

I also added a 15 mil board outline clearance rule to keep copper away from the edge. To be sure the rule was actually running, I set it to 200 mil first and got 70 violations, then set it back to 15 mil and reran the check to zero.
